# Build the PMU agent around alerts and outreach

A low-cost PMU job agent for Olga should not try to crawl job boards. Only one source in her target markets has a free, documented API with clear terms: Norway's NAV feed. Adzuna covers Spain and Poland. Every GCC board (Bayt, Naukrigulf, GulfTalent, Dubizzle, Qureos), along with Indeed and LinkedIn, can only be read through saved-search email alerts or through scraping, and scraping got blocked with HTTP 403/456 during this research. There are also very few PMU job ads: a "permanent makeup" search on Jooble UAE returned 211 hits and none of the first 20 was a PMU job. So the agent should be built for precision, plus a curated list of employers to contact directly, mainly Dubai medical clinics that already offer areola and scar micropigmentation, where her TBL Medical and restoration work stands out. Licensing decides most outcomes. In Dubai, PMU is a DHA-regulated procedure carried out under the **Beauty Therapist** licence title. She can get the registration before she has a job offer; only activation needs an employer. Every letter therefore has to describe her licence status using the official stage names and must not use titles like "cosmetologist". Published pay is modest, roughly **AED 4,000–7,000 basic plus commission** in Dubai and **SAR 10,000** in the one Riyadh PMU ad found. Many ads are traps for someone relocating ("own visa", "commission only"), so the red-flag filter matters as much as the keyword filter. The sources leave several points unresolved. The biggest is whether her Public Health (Cosmetology and Wellness) bachelor's degree and PhiBrows certificate meet DHA's qualification and training rules.

## Two free APIs, many email alerts, and very few PMU ads

The sources fall into three groups by how a machine can read them. Norway's **NAV pam-stilling-feed** is officially free to "anyone" who accepts the terms. A public test token rotates without notice, and you get a stable private token by emailing nav.team.arbeidsplassen@nav.no with a company ID and written acceptance of the terms. The feed has no keyword search: the agent downloads the full stream, filters `title` and `content_text` locally, supports ETag polling, and has to remove ads once they are marked INACTIVE ([NAV feed docs](https://navikt.github.io/pam-stilling-feed)). **Adzuna** offers a keyed REST search (`/v1/api/jobs/{country}/search`) with `what_exclude` ([Adzuna docs](https://developer.adzuna.com/docs/search)). Unofficial lists say it covers `es` and `pl` but not the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar or Norway, and its rate limits and terms were not verified ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/alternatives/adzuna.md)). **Jooble** has an API you have to request, aimed at portal operators, and runs ae/sa subdomains. Its results were nearly all noise: 211 UAE "permanent makeup" hits with no PMU roles in the first 20, and zero hits for "microblading" in Saudi Arabia ([Jooble AE](https://ae.jooble.org/jobs-permanent-makeup), [Jooble SA](https://sa.jooble.org/jobs-microblading)).

Everything else has no read API. Indeed shut down its publisher search API, and whether its RSS feeds still work in 2026 is unconfirmed ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/indeed-publisher-api.md)). Bayt returned 403, InfoJobs returned 456 and Pracuj.pl returned 403 to automated fetches. A serverless cron that scrapes them will break and probably breaches their terms. The practical route is saved-search email alerts on LinkedIn, Indeed AE/SA/QA, Bayt, Naukrigulf, GulfTalent, Qureos, FINN, InfoJobs, Pracuj and Jooble, routed into a Gmail label that the agent parses.

## DHA treats her as a Beauty Therapist, and registration can come before an offer

The DHA Standard for Non-Surgical Cosmetic Procedures (v2.1, effective 18 Feb 2025) lists "Semi-permanent Makeup, Micro-blading and removal… including eyebrow and lip tattoo" as permitted for **Beauty Therapists**. It is not permitted for nurses. Clause 7.11.3 allows beauty therapists to perform it in a salon without healthcare-professional supervision. The same standard requires an in-person training certificate from an accredited institution that states CPD hours, at least one year of experience for beauty therapists, and, in clinics, BLS and malpractice insurance ([DHA Standard](https://www.dha.gov.ae/uploads/122024/Standards%20for%20Non%20Surgical%20Cosmetic20241243161.pdf)). Clause 7.2 forbids licence holders from using titles such as "Aesthetician", "Cosmetologist" or "Beauty Expert". That affects how her degree is written in a Dubai CV: the degree can be named, but she should not present herself as a cosmetologist. The licensing manual sets the order of steps: Sheryan self-assessment, PQR, DataFlow PSV, CBT, then a **Professional Registration Certificate valid for one year**, which is "activated" by a DHA-licensed facility that hires her. PSV and CBT can run at the same time, and the good-standing certificate must be no older than six months ([DHA Licensing Manual](https://dha.gov.ae/uploads/122024/Manual%20for%20Licensing%20Healthcare%20professionals-202420241212775.pdf)). A consultancy reports that the Beauty Therapist CBT (BYT5862, 100 questions, 65% pass mark) has **no PMU domain**. It covers electrotherapy, peels and RF, so she will need to prepare. The same source reports DataFlow costs of about AED 935 plus VAT and a timeline of 3–6 weeks ([mydoctorlicense](https://mydoctorlicense.com/dha-license-for-aesthetician-beauty-therapist/)).

Abu Dhabi works differently. DMT Circular 6/2024 requires every salon microblading technician to hold a **QCC conformity certificate as a "Semi-Permanent Makeup Technician"**. A DOH Beauty Therapist licence matters only for clinic roles ([DMT Circular](https://pages.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/142F6293A49D46AF93FAD6A21EC5122A.ashx)). For Saudi Arabia and Qatar, no official source confirmed that SCFHS or MoPH licenses salon PMU. Saudi women's salons operate under a municipal licence ([Qanoniah](https://qanoniah.com/en/File/DmWqyAxNgpoP8NKoP26zvBXaE-Regulations-for-the-Practice-of-Women's-Beauty-Activities)). One training body says Qatar has no specific cosmetic-tattoo licence ([NIOB](https://www.nationalinstituteofbeauty.com/regulations/qatar/)), but MoPH arrested unlicensed "cosmetic medical" practitioners in a salon in January 2026 ([Peninsula](https://s.thepeninsula.qa/article/25/01/2026/health-ministry-apprehends-five-unlicensed-cosmetic-practitioners-in-beauty-salon)). For clinic roles in either country, the agent should ask the employer which licence applies rather than make a claim. In Europe, permits attach to the premises, not the practitioner. Norway requires municipal approval of the premises ([Bergen kommune](https://www.bergen.kommune.no/hvaskjer/tema/miljorettet-helseverns-temaside/helsevern-i-virksomheter/permanent-makeup-hudpleie-og-frisor)). Spain requires a regional hygienic-sanitary course and authorisation, and Real Decreto 488/2026 has added a 510-hour state micropigmentation certificate ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2026/06/19/pdfs/BOE-A-2026-13294.pdf)). Poland has Sanepid hygiene rules but no PMU licence ([GIS](https://www.gov.pl/attachment/eacabf2f-a954-4f38-999a-d74b7f10d96f)).

For phrasing, no recruiter source says how employers react to "in progress". The safe approach is to use DHA's own stage names and state exactly how far she has got. Before registration, the wording would be "DHA Beauty Therapist licensing in progress: Sheryan eligibility confirmed; DataFlow PSV submitted [month]; Prometric CBT booked [date]". After registration, it would be "DHA registered (Beauty Therapist), no. …, valid to [date]; ready for activation by employer". For Abu Dhabi salons, add "prepared to obtain QCC Semi-Permanent Makeup Technician certificate". She should never write "DHA licensed" before activation. Her December 2026 start date is about four weeks away, and a realistic registration path takes an estimated two to four months, so letters sent now should give her current stage and an expected registration date rather than imply she is ready to start immediately.

## Pay is AED 4–7k basic plus commission, and many ads should be filtered out

The PMU-specific pay data is thin, but it is consistent. Dubai ads offer **AED 4,000 + commission** for a five-year PMU artist and **AED 7,000 + commission** for a senior makeup artist at a luxury salon ([Qureos](https://app.qureos.com/jobs/senior-makeup-artist-luxury-beauty-salon-1776248567037)). Other ads offer "salary plus commission, or commission only" ([Indeed UAE](https://ae.indeed.com/q-permanent-makeup-l-dubai-free-zone-jobs.html)). Indeed puts the average Dubai makeup artist at AED 4,379/month ([Indeed](https://ae.indeed.com/career/makeup-artist/salaries/Dubai)). The single Riyadh data point is **SAR 10,000/month** at The Pink Tribe. Doha's general beautician ads pay only QAR 1,300–3,000, sometimes with housing, and one ad says "Asian candidates preferred" ([Qureos](https://app.qureos.com/jobs/all-rounder-beautician-asian-candidates-preferred-1772658367861)), so she should present herself as a PMU specialist or trainer, never as an all-rounder. UAE employers must pay visa costs, which run AED 3,000–10,000 for the employer ([Hyring](https://hyring.com/free-hr-toolkit/hr-glossary/employment-visa-uae), [SPC Free Zone](https://www.spcfz.ae/blogs/uae-employment-visa-requirements/)). No source gave commission percentages, or housing, flight or transport allowances, for any GCC beauty role. In Europe, Spanish salaried micropigmentation pay is around €1,500–2,500 gross/month ([InfoJobs](https://www.infojobs.net/oviedo/esteticista/of-i841b53832149e299bafddf1a58da44)). Norwegian treatment prices of NOK 4,000–6,000 per brow ([Klikk](https://www.klikk.no/mote/skjonnhet/microblading-2734035)) suggest that renting a room in an approved clinic could out-earn an AED 4,000 basic salary. The agent's negotiation checklist should therefore ask every employer for the commission percentage, a guaranteed minimum, visa and DHA activation costs, health insurance, housing and the flight.

On application norms, the sourced evidence covers what Dubai employers ask for: a DHA licence, an accredited PMU certificate, years of experience, a portfolio link, colour theory and English. Industry ads stress **healed** results ([Qureos](https://app.qureos.com/jobs/permanant-make-up-artist-microblading-lip-blush-eyeliner-1758139923996), [Glassdoor](https://static.glassdoor.be/Job/lash-tech-jobs-SRCH_KO0,9.htm)). A strong application opens with her DHA stage, then 14 years and 4,000+ clients across the techniques listed in DHA row 28, then areola and brow restoration in a medical clinic, then a healed-results portfolio. With Saudi employers, letters should say "semi-permanent makeup / micropigmentation" rather than "tattoo", given that the practice is religiously contested there ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/383832541_PERMANENT_MAKEUP_A_TATTOO_IMITATION_PROCEDURE_FROM_THE_SHARIAH_PERSPECTIVE)). Whether CV photos are expected, whether trade tests or model days are common, how WhatsApp-first contact works and what notice periods apply was not sourced, so the agent should not treat any of these as established norms.

Matching has to rest on technique terms. "Permanent" matches contract types ("fast stilling", "contrato indefinido", "umowa na stałe"), and "makeup" matches Sephora and bridal roles ([Jooble AE](https://ae.jooble.org/jobs-permanent-makeup)). Strong positives are **microblading, microshading, nanoblading, powder/ombré brows, lip blush, eyeliner tattoo, PMU, SPMU, semi-permanent makeup, micropigmentation, SMP, areola, paramedical/medical/cosmetic tattoo**, plus the local terms *mikropigmentacja, linergistka/linergista, makijaż permanentny, micropigmentación, micropigmentadora, dermopigmentación, kosmetisk tatovering*. Matching should be fuzzy, because a real ad spelled it "permanant". The Arabic, Norwegian and Spanish terms were not seen in any live ad. Medium-strength terms, which need a technique word in the body, are brow artist, lash and brow technician, "stylizacja brwi" and "beauty therapist (DHA)". "DHA license" boosts the score in Dubai. These terms should be excluded unless a strong positive is also present: MUA/bridal/wizażystka, beauty consultant, nail or lash-extension tech, receptionist, general beautician/kosmetyczka, body tattoo artist, Cronoshare quote requests and recruiter jobs. The scam rules come from MoHRE warnings. The agent should flag any fee asked of the candidate, work on a visit or tourist visa, requests for a passport or bank details before an offer, "no experience needed" and unusually high pay ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/mohre-warns-uae-jobseekers-of-fake-job-offers-and-visa-scams-1.500320699), [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/jobs/uae-warn-fake-offers-recruitment-scams)). For someone relocating, it should also exclude "own visa / husband's sponsorship", "freelance visa required", transferable iqama or NOC requirements, and nationality-restricted ads such as "British nationals based in Dubai" ([MNC Jobs Gulf](https://mncjobsgulf.com/jobs/spmu-artist-and-beautician-with-sales-expertise-british-nationals-based-in-dubai-dubai-1794847)). "Commission only" and unpaid trial days are sensible flags, but no source quantified how common they are.

## Conclusion

The bottleneck is not finding listings. It is being hireable when a listing appears. Dubai ads say "DHA license is a MUST", and DHA lets her get registration before any offer, so the most valuable step this month is running the Sheryan self-assessment. The result settles the largest unknown, her qualification fit, and it gives the agent a true, specific licence line to put in every letter. Her strongest edge is not pay but the work few artists offer: medical-clinic and areola or brow restoration. That matches a short list of Dubai surgical clinics far better than the salon market, where ads are more common but pay AED 4,000 plus unstated commission.

Two things in the evidence work against her and need deliberate handling. The December 2026 start date is earlier than a realistic licensing timeline, and the secondary markets mostly offer self-employment rather than jobs: Norway and Spain license premises, and Poland is saturated with newly trained artists. For Europe, the agent should look for approved clinics offering room rental or partnership, not employer vacancies. In Riyadh, the Saudization rules make trainer-plus-artist roles like Brau's the most realistic entry point. Her PhiBrows background is a concrete opening line with PhiStudio, Phiacademy and Black Pink Salon, even though the Phi master ladder does not hire outside artists directly as trainers ([Phi Academy](https://phi-academy.com/en-us/blog/how-to-become-master-at-phi-academy)).
